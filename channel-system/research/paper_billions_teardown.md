## The rules behind it (what our agents should copy)

1. **Publish within 24–48 hours** of a market event people are searching for.
2. **Title = the exact question people type.** Plain words, no jargon.
3. **0:00–0:08:** the biggest number plus a contradiction.
4. **By 0:20:** ask the title question out loud. **By 0:40:** give a short answer as a list of three. Then open the long story with a time and a place.
5. **Character skits about every 50–60 seconds** (about 15 in this video): two or three short lines that turn a fact into a joke ("I'm diversified. Five hundred companies." "Seven of them are a third of the pie.").
6. **Put the viewer in it** at least three times: their index fund, their 401(k), their power bill, their age.
7. **Every big number gets a human scale:** "eight cents of every dollar", "twice Amazon's building budget for the year", "$990 out of a $250,000 fund".
8. **Numbered lists** for complex causes ("Crack one... Crack four").
9. **Plant and pay off:** "Hold that thought", a quote or number from the opening that returns at the end.
10. **Tell the climax by the clock** when there is a single day of drama.
11. **Every claim has an owner:** a named person, a firm, a quote. About ten named sources here.
12. **Answer the title honestly**, add one twist, end with the next date to watch (a reason to come back).
13. **Length 12–15 minutes**, chapters of 1–3 minutes, each with a short title.

## What we do not copy

- Their character design, channel name, thumbnail layout or any line of the script.
- Caricatures of real people: their thumbnails draw recognizable executives. Our mascots are generic, never a real person.
- Accusations stated as fact. A "spy" title about a real company is defamation risk; we keep "alleged" language.
