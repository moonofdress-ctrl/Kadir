## Key numbers (with sources)
- **Headline price:** $1.95 billion, including $300 million of "anticipated cash tax benefits", net purchase price $1.65 billion, plus an undisclosed earnout. [P] S1, S2. The release says the Ellsworths created poppi, it was "discovered on Shark Tank by Rohan Oza and funded by CAVU Consumer Partners from their initial seed round to today".
- **What the $300M is:** no source I opened explains the tax mechanism. Do not explain it in the script beyond PepsiCo's own words ("anticipated cash tax benefits"). Unverified why it exists.
- **Earnout:** up to $300M (Bloomberg, S23), value PepsiCo carries: $278M (Dec 2025) to $117M (June 2026) to about $12M (5 Sep 2026). Bloomberg Intelligence's Ken Shea: "At this rate, it seems plausible that they may not pay any of the US$300mil". The release itself gives no amount (S1, S2); the $300M cap rests on Bloomberg. [W]
- **Shark Tank deal as aired:** ask $400,000 for 10% (valuation $4M); Oza: $400,000 for 25%. [W] S12, S13, S9, S15, S5. Closed as agreed: S6 and S9 both say he got a 25% stake for $400,000 and S1 says CAVU funded it from seed; one fan blog says it closed before airing. No document confirms the closing paperwork.
- **Stakes at sale:** not disclosed by any source I opened. Estimates only: S24 says Oza's stake was "worth nearly $500 million" at closing (that is just 25% of $1.95B, ignores dilution from the 2021 and 2022 rounds and the tax-benefit part; treat as a blog's arithmetic, not a fact); S6 says the Ellsworths received "over $100 million" (Fortune, no basis stated, single source, mark as estimate). S11 says the company gave equity to nearly all staff and 44 became millionaires (single source, interview claim).
- **Sales by year:** 2020 about $4M (S9; S11 says first year $3.5M); 2021 $22M, 2022 $52M, 2023 $200M, 2024 $515M per Entrepreneur's list (S11, the labels of the years are my reading of "first year, then..."); 2023 above $200M (S9) vs "topped $100 million" (CNBC, S19). 2024 about $500M (S5, company; S18 "$500 million in sales last year"). - **Rounds:** $13M (30 Aug 2021) led by CAVU; $25M (13 Dec 2022) led by CAVU; Looper (S14) says about $53M total capital by end of 2022. Stephen Ellsworth (S7): "as much as $25 million in extra capital" to get through Covid. No valuations disclosed.

## Why it matters to ordinary people
- The word "healthy" on a can. The suit said the can has about 2 g of prebiotic fiber and you would need more than four cans a day for any effect, which brings the sugar that cancels it (S4, an allegation, not a finding). Poppi denied it, removed the gut wording in late 2023, and says each can has 3 g fiber and 5 g sugar (S5).
- If you bought Poppi after 23 Jan 2020 you could claim $5 to $9 (claim deadline 26 Sep 2025). (S3, S4)
- Soda aisle: the big company bought the "healthier soda" instead of making it, and then made its own prebiotic Pepsi (S27 summary, 21 Jul 2025).
- The investor angle without advice: PepsiCo paid $1.95B headline, but the earnout PepsiCo itself values has collapsed (S23). We report, we do not say what to buy.

## The other side
- Poppi/Ellsworths: deny wrongdoing, settled to avoid cost and risk (S3, S5); "misinformation... by one of our competitors" on the vending machines (S20).
- PepsiCo: says Poppi had double-digit volume growth in Q3 2026 and gained share (S23); in Q1 2026 PepsiCo Beverages North America grew 9% (the blog S24 says 7 points came from Poppi: opinion source, check against PepsiCo's earnings release before using).

## History and the detail nobody noticed
- **Detail nobody noticed:** the settlement agreement is dated 5 Mar 2025, was filed in court on Fri 14 Mar 2025, and PepsiCo announced on Mon 17 Mar 2025: the lawsuit was priced and closed three days before the buyer spoke (S3, S1). Also, the court had paused the case on 16 Dec 2024, the same month PepsiCo's own Soulboost soda was reported dead (S3, S18).
- **Second detail:** the name. "Mother" could not be trademarked (S25), so the thing the Ellsworths pitched, the word on the bottle, was thrown away within two years; the PepsiCo release credits Oza's firm with funding it "from their initial seed round to today" (S1).

## The chain (honest answer to "How did Poppi make $1.95 billion from soda?")
Short answer: it did not make $1.95 billion in profit; it made a soda people wanted to be seen drinking, in a market where the big companies had nothing like it, and sold it for a headline number that is smaller after tax and earnout.
1. **The founders risked everything on a kitchen drink.** Credit cards, a sold car, their own plant, $75,000 in the bank at $20M revenue (S6, S7, S11).
2. **A Shark paid for the brand, not just the drink.** Oza's $400,000 for 25% came with the order to scrap the name and package; "this liquid, that's it" (S9, S25).
3. **Covid and TikTok turned a repackaged soda into a trend.** Sales went from about $4M (2020) to above $100M or $200M (2023) to about $500M (2024); influencer money and CAVU's rounds paid for shelves (S9, S11, S16, S17, S19).
4. **Big soda could not build it.** PepsiCo's own Soulboost was cancelled, Coca-Cola launched Simply Pop, Olipop was valued at $1.85B (S18, S19).
5. **The legal risk was settled just before the sale.** $8.9M, no admission, signed 5 Mar 2025, announced 17 Mar (S3, S1).
6. **The price is a headline.** $1.95B includes $300M of tax benefits; net $1.65B; the earnout of up to $300M is now valued by PepsiCo at about $12M (S1, S23).
