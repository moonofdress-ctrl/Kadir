# Opening hook variants (first ~20 seconds)

Three alternatives for the cold open. Each replaces the first four narration lines of
Part 1 Step 1 in `script_master.md` (current version = Hook A). Everything after
"Here's a typical apparel seller..." stays the same.

Test idea: publish with A, then use YouTube's "Test & compare" on thumbnails first.
Swap hooks only between uploads, not on a live video.

## Hook A · The number (current)
Visual: black frame, "$100" slams in, then the receipt starts printing.

> Every time you spend one hundred dollars with a seller on Amazon... about fifty-two of those dollars never reach the seller.
> Not the brand. Not the factory. Amazon.
> Last year, a single line in Amazon's annual report — advertising — brought in sixty-eight point six billion dollars. That's roughly one hundred and eighty-eight million dollars... every single day.
> In the next nine minutes, we'll follow one hundred dollars of sales — dollar by dollar — through every fee, every auction, and every return. And by the end, you'll see why the U.S. government has taken Amazon to court over it — twice... and why, according to the FTC, you may be paying this tax even if you never shop on Amazon at all.

Why it works: one concrete number in the first sentence, payoff promised at 0:20.

## Hook B · The courtroom
Visual: evidence desk, the 2026 complaint lands on the table, "ALLEGED" stamp.

> In August 2026, the U.S. government sued Amazon... again.
> This time, the claim is simple. The FTC says Amazon secretly overcharged the businesses that advertise on its site — by more than twenty billion dollars.
> Amazon says that's wrong. But to understand the fight, you need to see one thing first: what actually happens to one hundred dollars when you click "Buy Now."
> Because about fifty-two of those dollars never reach the seller. And according to the FTC, you may be paying for that even if you never shop on Amazon at all.

Why it works: news hook and conflict first. Best if the case is in the headlines the week you publish.

## Hook C · The viewer's own phone
Visual: your phone screen recording of an Amazon search, "Sponsored" labels counted on screen.

> Open Amazon on your phone and search for anything. A dress. A phone charger. Dog food.
> Now count how many results say "Sponsored" before you reach one that doesn't.
> Every one of those slots was bought. And the money behind them added up to sixty-eight point six billion dollars last year — more than half the combined revenue of every daily and weekly newspaper on Earth.
> That money comes out of the price you pay. In the next nine minutes, we'll follow one hundred dollars of sales, dollar by dollar... and by the end, you'll see why the FTC says you're paying this tax even when you shop somewhere else.

Why it works: the viewer can verify it in ten seconds, which builds trust. Needs your own screen recording.

## Rules all three follow
- First sentence under 20 words, with the subject on screen at 0:00.
- A specific number inside the first 10 seconds.
- A promise of what the viewer gets by the end, stated before 0:25.
- FTC claims worded as claims ("the FTC says", "according to the FTC").
